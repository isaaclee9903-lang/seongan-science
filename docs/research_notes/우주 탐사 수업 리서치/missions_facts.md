## 1. 파커 태양 탐사선 (Parker Solar Probe)

### Takeaway
On 24 Dec 2024 Parker passed 6.1 million km from the Sun's surface at about 692,000 km/h. That pass set two records: the closest any human-made object has come to a star, and the fastest any human-made object has moved. Since then it has repeated that distance about every 88 days, with the 28th perihelion in June 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Closest approach on 24 Dec 2024: 3.8 million miles (about 6.1 million km) from the Sun's surface, the closest any human-made object has come to a star — [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/heliophysics/nasas-parker-solar-probe-makes-history-with-closest-pass-to-sun/); [JHUAPL](https://www.jhuapl.edu/news/news-releases/241227-parker-closest-approach)
- Speed about 430,000 mph (692,000 km/h), faster than any human-made object. Another NASA release converts the same speed to 687,000 km/h, so the metric figure varies slightly by source — [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/heliophysics/nasas-parker-solar-probe-makes-history-with-closest-pass-to-sun/)
- The spacecraft was out of contact during the pass. A beacon tone received at APL late on 26 Dec confirmed it was safe and working normally — [NASA blog](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/parker-solar-probe/2024/12/27/nasas-parker-solar-probe-reports-successful-closest-approach-to-sun/)
- Heat shield temperatures:
  - The shield was designed to withstand about 2,600°F (about 1,430°C), "hot enough to melt steel".
  - At closest approach it was expected to reach about 1,800°F (980–1,000°C).
  - The instruments behind it stay near room temperature, about 85°F (29°C).
  - Source: [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/heliophysics/nasas-parker-solar-probe-makes-history-with-closest-pass-to-sun/); [NASA #3point8 page](https://science.nasa.gov/parker-solar-probe/3point8-campaign/)
- Popular claim correction: "the shield is 2,500°F" is wrong. The design limit is about 1,430°C, and about 1,000°C was expected at the record pass.
- Closest-ever images of the Sun were released in July 2025 — [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/heliophysics/nasas-parker-solar-probe-snaps-closest-ever-images-to-sun/)
- The team won the 2024 Collier Trophy — [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/heliophysics/nasas-parker-solar-probe-team-wins-2024-collier-trophy/)
- 2026 perihelia:
  - #27 was scheduled for 11 Mar 2026 — [UCAR WHPI campaign](https://whpi.hao.ucar.edu/whpi_campaign-psp27p.php)
  - #28 was reported on 8 June 2026, again at about 430,000 mph — [UCAR WHPI](https://whpi.hao.ucar.edu/whpi_campaign-psp28p.php); secondary coverage
  - #29 was forecast for 4 Sep 2026 — [UCAR WHPI](https://whpi.hao.ucar.edu/whpi_campaign-psp29p.php)
  - The 9.86 solar-radii perihelion repeats every 88 days — [EGU26 abstract](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-22305.html)
- Mission overview — [Parker Solar Probe (APL)](https://parkersolarprobe.jhuapl.edu/The-Mission/index.php)

### Inferences
- For students: "Seoul to Busan (about 400 km) in about 2 seconds". This follows from 692,000 km/h ≈ 192 km/s.
- For students: "The front is hot enough to melt steel, but the back is room temperature." This is a strong hook for a heat shield/shadow visual.

### Gaps
- Launch date 12 Aug 2018: not confirmed on a fetched page this session. It is on the [APL mission page](https://parkersolarprobe.jhuapl.edu/The-Mission/index.php).
- Unverified, from background knowledge; check before use:
  - It was the first NASA spacecraft named after a living person, Eugene Parker.
  - Parker watched the launch and died in March 2022.
  - The heat shield is about 11.4 cm (4.5 in) of carbon composite foam.
- Outcome of perihelion #29 (Sep 2026) and any late-2026 extended-mission news: not found.

## 2. 솔라 오비터 (Solar Orbiter, ESA–NASA)

### Takeaway
In 2020 Solar Orbiter found "campfires", millions of tiny flares. In 2025, after tilting its orbit, it took the first-ever images of the Sun's pole.

### Cited Findings
- "Campfires" come from the first EUI images, taken 30 May 2020 near the first perihelion. The instrument's lead scientist called them the "little nephews of solar flares", at least a million, perhaps a billion times smaller — [ESA](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Solar_Orbiter/Solar_Orbiter_s_first_images_reveal_campfires_on_the_Sun)
- World-first views of the Sun's south pole came in March 2025, at about 15–17° below the solar equator. These are the first images ever of a solar pole — [ESA](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Solar_Orbiter/Solar_Orbiter_gets_world-first_views_of_the_Sun_s_poles); [ESA image](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2025/06/Solar_Orbiter_s_world-first_views_of_the_Sun_s_south_pole)
- At the south pole, both north and south magnetic polarities were found mixed together, a "magnetic mess" — [ESA](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Solar_Orbiter/Solar_Orbiter_gets_world-first_views_of_the_Sun_s_poles)
- Why it tilts toward the poles — [ESA](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2025/06/Why_Solar_Orbiter_is_angling_towards_the_Sun_s_poles)

### Inferences
- Every Sun image before 2025 was taken from near the equator plane. Students have never seen the "top" of the Sun.

### Gaps
- Launch date (10 Feb 2020), and that the tilt will increase to about 24° (2027) and 33° (2029): unverified this session.
- Campfires were not reported at the poles. Do not combine the two findings.

## 3. 보이저 2호 (and 보이저 1호 for context)

### Takeaway
Voyager 2 is still the only spacecraft ever to visit Uranus (1986) and Neptune (1989). Both Voyagers are now in interstellar space and are being kept alive by switching instruments off one by one. Voyager 1 lost another instrument in April 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Voyager 2 key dates:
  - Launched 20 Aug 1977, 16 days before Voyager 1. That is why it is "2" yet launched first.
  - Jupiter 9 Jul 1979, Saturn 25–26 Aug 1981, Uranus 24 Jan 1986, Neptune 25 Aug 1989.
  - Source: [NASA Science – Voyager 2](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-2/)
- Voyager 2 is "the only spacecraft to visit Uranus and Neptune" and the only one to study all four giant planets up close — [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-2/)
- Voyager 2 discoveries — [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-2/)
  - Uranus: 10 new moons and 2 new rings
  - Neptune: 5 or 6 new moons (the page gives both) and 4 rings
  - Jupiter: 1 new moon
- Interstellar entry date conflicts on the NASA page: the Key Dates list says 25 Nov 2018, but the text says Voyager 2 joined Voyager 1 on 10 Dec 2018 — [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-2/)
- 2023 antenna incident — [JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-mission-update-voyager-2-communications-pause/); [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/jpl/nasa-contacts-voyager-2-using-upgraded-deep-space-network-dish/)
  - On 21 Jul 2023, routine commands accidentally pointed the antenna 2 degrees away from Earth.
  - Canberra's Deep Space Network dish sent an interstellar "shout" over 12.3 billion miles (about 19.9 billion km).
  - The one-way light time was 18.5 hours, so the team waited 37 hours to learn whether it worked.
  - Full contact was restored by 4 Aug 2023.
- Power status in 2025–2026 — [NASA Voyager blog, 17 Apr 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/voyager/2026/04/17/nasa-shuts-off-instrument-on-voyager-1-to-keep-spacecraft-operating/); [JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-shuts-off-instrument-on-voyager-1-to-keep-spacecraft-operating/)
  - Each Voyager loses about 4 W of power per year.
  - Voyager 2's LECP instrument was shut off in March 2025.
  - Voyager 1's LECP was shut off on 17 Apr 2026, after an unexpected power drop that followed a roll maneuver on 27 Feb 2026.
  - Of 10 instruments per spacecraft, 7 have been shut off.
  - Voyager 1 keeps 2 science instruments: plasma wave and magnetic field.
  - Voyager 1 is more than 25 billion km from Earth, and commands take about 23 hours to arrive.
  - A power-saving test called the "Big Bang" was planned on Voyager 2 for May–June 2026.
- Golden Record — [NASA greetings page](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/golden-record-contents/greetings/)
  - It carries spoken greetings in 55 languages, and Korean is one of them.
  - NASA gives the English meaning of the Korean greeting as "How are you?"
  - The greetings were assembled by Linda Salzman Sagan and recorded mainly at Cornell University.
- Popular claim correction: the Korean greeting is often quoted as "안녕하세요". NASA's translation is "How are you?", which suggests a phrase like "안녕하셨어요?" or "안녕하십니까?" rather than a plain "안녕하세요". The exact Korean wording could not be confirmed from NASA's page. Listen to the audio before quoting it to students.

### Inferences
- Voyager 1's signal takes about 23 hours one way. For students: "If you said 'hello' today, you'd hear 'hi' back the day after tomorrow."
- Voyager 2 has been "closing its eyes one by one". That framing is useful for a "why spacecraft die slowly" discussion: the nuclear battery weakens about 4 W per year.

### Gaps
- Voyager 2's current distance (2026) and its remaining instrument list: not stated on the fetched page. Check the live counter at [Voyager mission status](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/where-are-voyager-1-and-voyager-2-now/).
- Unverified, from background knowledge; source with [NASA Science Voyager 1](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-1/):
  - Pale Blue Dot (창백한 푸른 점): Voyager 1, 14 Feb 1990, taken from about 6 billion km away.
  - Voyager 1 entered interstellar space in Aug 2012.
- Unverified Golden Record counts: 116 images, 90 minutes of music, about 27 pieces.
- Interstellar entry dates for Voyager 2 conflict (25 Nov vs 10 Dec 2018). A widely cited older NASA release gives 5 Nov 2018 (unverified here). Use "2018년 11~12월" or just "2018년".

## 4. 로제타 & 필레 (Rosetta & Philae, ESA)

### Takeaway
Rosetta was the first spacecraft to orbit a comet, and Philae was the first to land on one. Philae's harpoons failed and it bounced about 1 km up, flying roughly 2 hours. It ended up in a dark crack, and Rosetta found it only on 2 Sep 2016, four weeks before Rosetta itself was crashed into the comet on 30 Sep 2016.

### Cited Findings
- 67P was the "first comet to be orbited and landed upon by robotic spacecraft from Earth" — [NASA Science – 67P](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/67p-churyumov-gerasimenko/)
- Rosetta arrived in Aug 2014, and the mission ended with a controlled impact on the comet on 30 Sep 2016 — [NASA Science – 67P](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/67p-churyumov-gerasimenko/)
- Rosetta discoveries — [NASA Science – 67P](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/67p-churyumov-gerasimenko/)
  - The comet's water differs significantly from Earth's water.
  - First detection of molecular oxygen and nitrogen at a comet.
  - Glycine (an amino acid) and phosphorus.
  - The nucleus is less dense than water.
- Landing: Philae first touched down at Agilkia on 12 Nov 2014. It bounced, flew about 2 more hours, and came to rest at "Abydos" on the smaller lobe — [ESA Philae found](https://sci.esa.int/web/rosetta/-/58220-philae-found); [ESA blog](https://blogs.esa.int/rosetta/2016/09/05/philae-found/)
- The first bounce went about 1 km high. Both anchoring systems failed: the harpoons and a downward-pushing thruster — [Sky & Telescope](https://skyandtelescope.org/astronomy-news/esa-locates-philae-lander/); [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/after-two-years-searching-comet-lander-philae-finally-found-180960350/). These are secondary sources consistent with ESA.
- Philae was found in OSIRIS images taken 2 Sep 2016 from 2.7 km away, and ESA announced it on 5 Sep 2016 — [ESA](https://sci.esa.int/web/rosetta/-/58220-philae-found); [ESA Kids](https://www.esa.int/kids/en/learn/Our_Universe/Comets_and_meteors/Rosetta_has_found_Philae)
  - It was wedged in a dark crack, tipped on its side, with 2 of its 3 legs visible.
  - Its orientation explains why communication was so hard.
  - An ESA manager said "We were beginning to think that Philae would remain lost forever."

### Inferences
- Story arc for students: lost lander, then found in a photo, then the mothership "lands" next to it.

### Gaps
- Unverified, from background knowledge; check the ESA Rosetta pages:
  - Launch on 2 Mar 2004 and arrival on 6 Aug 2014. The trip of about 10 years included 3 Earth flybys and 1 Mars flyby.
  - Philae worked about 60 hours on its battery, then went silent. It "woke up" briefly on 13 Jun 2015.
  - The comet is about 4 km long.
- "Rubber duck" shape: a common media nickname, but no official page with that wording was confirmed. Present it as "생김새가 고무 오리를 닮았다고 해서 붙은 별명".

## 5. 딥 임팩트 (Deep Impact, NASA)

### Takeaway
On 4 Jul 2005 a 370 kg impactor hit comet Tempel 1 at about 10.2 km/s on purpose. It was the first time humans dug into a comet, and Stardust-NExT revisited the scar in 2011.

### Cited Findings
- Impactor released 3 Jul 2005, impact 4 Jul 2005, with a bright flash at impact — [JPL Deep Impact](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/missions/deep-impact/)
- Impactor about 370–372 kg, about half copper, at 10.2–10.3 km/s. These are research-paper values that vary slightly — [arXiv astro-ph/0605389](https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0605389)
- Stardust-NExT flew past Tempel 1 on 14 Feb 2011 at about 178 km and imaged the impact site — [NASA image](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/tempel-1-impact-site/); [JPL news](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-releases-images-of-man-made-crater-on-comet/)
- Crater size conflicts across sources:
  - NASA public release: about 150 m wide — [JPL news](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-releases-images-of-man-made-crater-on-comet/)
  - Detailed analysis: a faint crater about 49 ± 12 m, "tentatively identified" — [Stardust-NExT, Richardson & Melosh](https://stardustnext.jpl.nasa.gov/science/stardust_next_Richardson_Melosh.html)

### Inferences
- Use "축구장 정도 크기의 구덩이(약 150 m, NASA 발표)" only if hedged. The safer wording is "작은 인공 구덩이가 생겼다".
- Deep Impact (2005) to DART (2022) is a nice storyline: "comet poke" leads to "asteroid push".

### Gaps
- The speed in km/h (about 37,000 km/h) is my conversion, not a sourced figure.

## 6. 다트 (DART) & 헤라 (Hera)

### Takeaway
DART deliberately crashed into the small moonlet Dimorphos on 26 Sep 2022. It shortened the moonlet's 11 h 55 min orbit by about 33 minutes, more than 25 times the 73-second minimum NASA had set as "success". It was the first time humans changed the motion of a natural body in space.

### Cited Findings
- First confirmation on 11 Oct 2022 — [JHUAPL](https://www.jhuapl.edu/news/news-releases/221011-dart-mission-impact-changed-asteroids-motion-in-space)
  - The orbit went from 11 h 55 min to 11 h 23 min, about 32 ± 2 min shorter.
  - The minimum success benchmark was 73 seconds, beaten by more than 25 times.
- Refined value: about 33 minutes, given as −33.0 ± 1.0 min — [PMC paper](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10115635/); [NASA final technical report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230015804)
- A 2024 JPL study found the orbit is 33 min 15 s shorter and that Dimorphos' shape also changed — [JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-study-asteroids-orbit-shape-changed-after-dart-impact/)
- Debris (ejecta) thrown off by the impact added a lot of extra push beyond DART's own momentum — [PMC paper](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10115635/)
- Hera (ESA) — [ESA](https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Hera/ESA_s_Hera_targets_early_arrival_at_Didymos_asteroids)
  - Launched 7 Oct 2024, with a Mars flyby in March 2025.
  - It now targets arrival at Didymos in **November 2026**, a month earlier than planned.
  - It carries 2 CubeSats, Juventas and Milani.
- Overview — [NASA Science Didymos](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/asteroids/didymos/)
- Popular claim correction: "32 minutes" is the 2022 first estimate. Use "약 33분" today.

### Inferences
- As of 11 Oct 2026, Hera has **not yet arrived**; arrival is expected next month. The lesson can say "2026년 11월 도착 예정". If the lesson runs after Nov 2026, re-check.

### Gaps
- DART spacecraft mass (about 570–610 kg at impact) and speed (about 6.1 km/s, about 22,000 km/h): unverified this session. Check [NASA DART](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/dart/).
- Hera arrival confirmation (Nov 2026): not yet possible.

## 7. 큐리오시티 (Curiosity, NASA)

### Takeaway
Curiosity, a car-sized rover, was lowered onto Mars by a rocket-powered "sky crane" on 6 Aug 2012 (US EDT), inside Gale Crater. On its first birthday, NASA engineers made its SAM instrument "sing" Happy Birthday.

### Cited Findings
- Landed in Gale Crater on 6 Aug 2012 EDT (5 Aug PDT) using the sky crane — [JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/heres-how-curiositys-sky-crane-changed-the-way-nasa-explores-mars/)
  - The rover was too heavy for airbags.
  - At about 18 m (60 ft) above the ground, the descent stage hovered and lowered the rover on 3 nylon ropes.
- Happy Birthday (Aug 2013) — [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/happy-birthday-curiosity/); [NASA SVS](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/11331)
  - Goddard engineers used the SAM instrument to "sing" Happy Birthday by vibrating it at musical frequencies.
  - The video was recorded with SAM's identical test unit on Earth.
- Popular claim correction: "Curiosity sang happy birthday to itself, alone on Mars" is a popular simplification. The NASA video shows engineers doing it, recorded on Earth's twin unit. A JPL podcast states the rover sang on Mars ([JPL podcast](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/podcasts/on-a-mission-season-1/season-4-mars-rovers/episode-5-a-ton-of-curiosity/)), so sources conflict. Safest wording: "NASA는 1주년에 큐리오시티의 분석 장비를 진동시켜 생일 축하 노래 음을 내게 했다".

### Inferences
- The sky crane changed how NASA lands: Perseverance (2021) used it too. That is implied by the JPL article title.

### Gaps
- Unverified this session; check the [Curiosity mission page](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/msl-curiosity/):
  - "7 minutes of terror": entry to touchdown takes about 7 minutes, and the radio delay is longer than that, so the landing happened with no human control.
  - Selfies are stitched from many photos taken with the arm-mounted MAHLI camera, which is why the arm "disappears".
  - Wheel damage: holes and tears from sharp rocks, noticed in 2013.
  - Gale crater findings: an ancient lake and habitable environment (2013–2014), and organic molecules (2018).
  - Still operating in 2026.

## 8. 주노 (Juno, NASA)

### Takeaway
Juno launched in Aug 2011 and has orbited Jupiter since 2016. It carries 3 aluminum LEGO figures. Its NASA-funded extended mission ran only to 30 Sep 2025, and its status after that is **officially unclear in the sources found**.

### Cited Findings
- Launched in Aug 2011 on an Atlas V and reached Jupiter in 2016 — [NASA history](https://www.nasa.gov/history/10-years-ago-juno-launched-to-observe-jupiter/)
- Three LEGO minifigures, made of aluminum rather than plastic so they survive the flight — [JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/juno-spacecraft-to-carry-three-figurines-to-jupiter-orbit/); [NASA photojournal](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/lego-figurines-aboard-juno/)
  - Galileo with a telescope.
  - Jupiter with a lightning bolt.
  - Juno with a magnifying glass.
- An aluminum plaque carries Galileo's self-portrait and his handwritten notes on discovering Jupiter's moons — [JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/juno-spacecraft-to-carry-three-figurines-to-jupiter-orbit/)
- Originally planned as a 20-month mission; it made close flybys of Ganymede, Europa and Io — [Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/missions/nasas-juno-probe-orbiting-jupiter-may-have-come-to-an-end-but-no-one-can-confirm)
- The extended mission expired 30 Sep 2025, during a US government shutdown. NASA could not confirm whether Juno was still operating — [Space.com, Oct 2025](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/missions/nasas-juno-probe-orbiting-jupiter-may-have-come-to-an-end-but-no-one-can-confirm)
- The JPL mission page still lists the status as "Current" — [JPL Juno](https://jpl.nasa.gov/missions/juno/)
- Planned end: a deliberate plunge into Jupiter, to avoid contaminating moons such as Europa (planetary protection) — [Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/missions/nasas-juno-probe-orbiting-jupiter-may-have-come-to-an-end-but-no-one-can-confirm)

### Inferences
- Juno's status in Oct 2026 is UNVERIFIED. In the lesson, say "2016년부터 목성을 돌며 탐사했다" and avoid "지금도 탐사 중".

### Gaps
- The "solar-powered record" was not confirmed this session. Background knowledge says Juno was the farthest solar-powered spacecraft until it was surpassed by ESA's JUICE/Lucy, depending on the definition (unverified, so avoid it).
- No official 2026 Juno status statement was found.

## 9. 카시니-하위헌스 (Cassini-Huygens, NASA/ESA/ASI)

### Takeaway
ESA's Huygens probe landed on Titan in 2005, the first landing in the outer Solar System. Cassini found that Enceladus might host habitable conditions, so in 2017 NASA steered nearly-empty Cassini into Saturn on purpose to protect those moons from Earth microbes.

### Cited Findings
- Huygens was the "first ever landing in the outer Solar System" — [ESA Kids](https://www.esa.int/kids/en/learn/Our_Universe/Planets_and_moons/Countdown_to_Cassini_s_grand_finale)
  - It found that Titan has weather.
  - The surface temperature is about −180°C.
  - Titan has lakes of methane.
- Grand Finale: Cassini was out of fuel after two decades. NASA decided in 2010 to end with a plunge into Saturn — [NASA Science Grand Finale](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/cassini/grand-finale/overview/)
  - Reason: Earth microbes might still survive onboard, and Enceladus and Titan show potential habitable or prebiotic environments.
  - Last Titan flyby on 11 Sep 2017 set up the final dive — [ESA](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Cassini-Huygens/Cassini_concludes_pioneering_mission_at_Saturn)
  - The mission ended in Saturn's atmosphere — [NASA release](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasas-cassini-spacecraft-ends-its-historic-exploration-of-saturn/)
- On 22 Apr 2017 a Titan flyby at 979 km set Cassini on its ring-gap orbits — [JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-cassini-mission-prepares-for-grand-finale-at-saturn/)

### Inferences
- "Most distant landing ever" is commonly said. ESA's wording confirmed here is "first landing in the outer Solar System". Use that, or "지금까지 가장 먼 곳에 착륙" only after checking.

### Gaps
- Unverified dates: Huygens landed on 14 Jan 2005, and the plunge was on 15 Sep 2017.
- Enceladus geysers (plumes of water ice from a subsurface ocean) are referenced implicitly ("ocean-bearing moon Enceladus", [ESA](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Cassini-Huygens/Cassini_concludes_pioneering_mission_at_Saturn)), but the plume discovery date (2005) is unverified.

## 10. 아폴로 계획 (Apollo program) — checking the textbook claim

### Takeaway
The claim "아폴로 계획에서 총 11차례 유인 우주선을 달에 발사, 6번 착륙" is half right. Apollo 7–17 were 11 crewed flights and 6 landed (11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17). But Apollo 7 and 9 stayed in Earth orbit, so only **9** crewed flights actually went to the Moon.

### Cited Findings
- Six landing missions: 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17 — [NSSDCA Apollo](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/lunar/apollo.html); [NASA Apollo missions](https://www.nasa.gov/specials/apollo50th/missions.html)
- Non-landing crewed flights:
  - Apollo 7 and 9: Earth-orbit tests.
  - Apollo 8 and 10: orbited the Moon.
  - Apollo 13: lunar flyby after an accident, no landing.
  - Source: [NSSDCA](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/lunar/apollo.html); [Apollo 7 record](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/nmc/spacecraft/display.action?id=1968-089A)
- 12 American astronauts walked on the Moon (1969–1972), 2 per landing — [NASA Science Moonwalkers](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-walkers/)
- Apollo 17 was the last landing, at Taurus-Littrow on 11 Dec 1972 — [NASA Apollo 17](https://solarsystem.nasa.gov/missions/apollo-17/in-depth)
- Apollo brought back 382 kg of rock and soil — [NASA Science Moon exploration](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/exploration/)
- NASA's kids page counts 11 Apollo flights — [NASA Grades 5-8](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-kids-and-students/what-was-the-apollo-program-grades-5-8/)

### Inferences
- Suggested accurate Korean phrasing: "아폴로 계획에서는 11번 유인 우주선을 발사했고, 그중 9번이 달까지 갔으며 6번 달에 착륙해 12명이 달을 걸었다." If you must keep the textbook phrase, note that "달에 발사" loosely includes the 2 Earth-orbit test flights.

### Gaps
- Apollo 1 (1967 ground-test fire, crew lost) was never launched and is not counted. This is background knowledge, unverified here.

## Cross-cutting: "why it mattered to humanity" (one line each, drafted from cited findings above)
- 파커: the first spacecraft to "touch" a star's atmosphere. It helps forecast space weather that can affect satellites.
- 솔라 오비터: the first look at the Sun's poles, where the Sun's magnetic cycle is decided.
- 보이저 2호: the only close-up look humanity has of Uranus and Neptune. Both Voyagers carry Earth's greeting into interstellar space.
- 로제타·필레: the first comet landing. It found amino acid (glycine) and phosphorus, building blocks linked to life.
- 딥 임팩트: the first look inside a comet by hitting it.
- 다트: the first proof that humans can change an asteroid's path, a planetary defense test.
- 큐리오시티: showed Mars once had conditions suitable for life (needs source; see Gaps).
- 주노: revealed Jupiter's interior and moons up close.
- 카시니-하위헌스: found moons that might be habitable, and ended itself to protect them.
- 아폴로: the only time humans have walked on another world (12 people).
