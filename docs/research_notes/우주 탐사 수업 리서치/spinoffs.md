# 일상생활 속 우주 탐사 기술 (NASA 스핀오프) 사실 확인

Fact-check of the grade-8 textbook figure "일상생활에서 사용하는 우주 탐사 기술". Verdict scale:
- **GENUINE**: the product came out of NASA (or NASA-contract) work for space or aeronautics.
- **PARTIAL**: the thing existed already. NASA work improved it, or NASA technology went into one version of it.
- **MYTH**: NASA did not invent it, and space use only made it famous.
- **UNVERIFIED**: I found no reliable source.

Note: NASA itself defines "spinoff" loosely. A Spinoff editor said "We are fairly liberal with our application of the term spinoff" ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna43772079)). Many items below are PARTIAL rather than "invented by NASA". The safest wording for the lesson is "우주 탐사 기술이 쓰였다/발전시켰다", not "NASA가 발명했다".

## Q1. Textbook items: verdict, kid-friendly story, year, source

### Takeaway
Most textbook items have a real, documented NASA link. Memory foam, scratch-resistant lens coating, the ear thermometer, the CMOS camera sensor, clear braces, the anti-gravity treadmill and seismic dampers are the strongest cases. The smoke detector, cordless vacuum, GPS, cochlear implant and functional clothing are only "improved / partly", and the textbook wording should be softened for them. The microwave oven and wireless headphones have no NASA origin I could verify.

### Cited Findings

**메모리폼 침대 (memory foam): GENUINE**
- Year: NASA Ames contract to Stencel Aero Engineering in 1966. Charles Yost made an open-cell "slow spring back" foam for safer, more comfortable airplane seats. It was sold as "temper foam" from 1969, and the mass-produced Tempur foam came in 1991. — [NASA Spinoff 2005](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/Spinoff2005/ch_6.html); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_spin-off_technologies)
- NASA's FAQ says it was "developed under a NASA contract in the late 1960s." — [NASA Spinoff FAQ](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/faq)
- Correction: it was made for **airplane seats** (aeronautics), not for astronauts' beds. It was later used in Space Shuttle seats. — [NASA Spinoff 2005](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/Spinoff2005/ch_6.html)
- Kid story: NASA wanted airplane seats that soften crashes and stay comfortable on long flights. The engineer invented a foam that sinks slowly and then springs back, and today it is in pillows and mattresses.

**기능성 의류 (functional / temperature-regulating clothing): PARTIAL (NASA-funded research, never flew)**
- 1987–1988: NASA Johnson Space Center gave SBIR contracts to Triangle Research & Development. The goal was phase-change-material fabric to keep spacesuit gloves at a steady temperature. The glove inserts never went to space. The patent went to Gateway Technologies, later Outlast Technologies (1997), which put it into clothes, bedding and chairs. — [NASA Spinoff "Feeling Hot, Staying Cool"](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/Feeling-Hot-Staying-Cool); [NASA Spinoff](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/10351)
- Related: NASA Langley wind tunnels and flow software helped Speedo design the LZR Racer swimsuit, about 2008. — [Wikipedia summary citing Spinoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_spin-off_technologies)
- Caution: "기능성 의류" in general (moisture-wicking sportswear, etc.) did not come from NASA. Only these specific cases are documented.
- Kid story: NASA paid scientists to make glove fabric that soaks up heat by melting tiny wax beads and gives the heat back when it gets cold. The gloves never flew, but the fabric is now in jackets and blankets.

**코팅 안경 렌즈 (scratch-resistant lens coating): GENUINE**
- Ted Wydeven at NASA Ames found a thin plastic coating method while working on spacecraft water purification. NASA turned it into a scratch-resistant coating for astronaut helmet visors. Foster Grant licensed it in 1983 and sold lenses from 1984. — [Space Foundation Hall of Fame](https://www.spacefoundation.org/space_technology_hal/scratch-resistant-lenses/); [NASA Spinoff](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9031)
- NASA FAQ: yes, a NASA-created coating. — [NASA Spinoff FAQ](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/faq)
- Kid story: Astronaut helmet visors are plastic and scratch easily, so NASA invented a tough thin coating. Glasses makers borrowed it so plastic glasses scratch less.

**반중력 러닝머신 (AlterG anti-gravity treadmill): GENUINE**
- Robert Whalen at NASA Ames wanted to use air pressure to "add" Earth-like weight for astronauts exercising in space. Reversing the idea removes weight instead. He patented it in 1992, and AlterG licensed it in 2005. It can take away up to about 80% of body weight for rehab. — [NASA Spinoff](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/11541); [Tech Briefs](https://www.techbriefs.com/component/content/article/52891-space-tech-gives-treadmill-users-a-boost)
- Inducted into the Space Foundation Space Technology Hall of Fame (listed as 2026). — [Space Foundation](https://www.spacefoundation.org/space_technology_hal/microgravity-treadmill/)
- Kid story: In space, astronauts float and their bones and muscles weaken. NASA tried using air pressure to push them down onto the treadmill. Flip the idea, push air up, and an injured runner on Earth feels lighter.

**시력 교정 수술 장치 (LASIK eye-tracking): PARTIAL (NASA tech in one laser system)**
- In the 1980s NASA developed LADAR (laser radar) so spacecraft could dock automatically. That tracking technology went into the LADARVision 4000 laser eye-surgery system, approved in 1998. It tracks eye movement 4,000 times per second. — [NASA Spinoff 2003 hm_1, cited in Wikipedia](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/spinoff2003/hm_1.html); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_spin-off_technologies)
- Caution: LASIK surgery itself was not invented by NASA. Only the eye-tracking in this one system is a spinoff.
- Kid story: A spacecraft docking in space must keep "watching" its target with a laser. The same idea lets an eye-surgery laser follow tiny eye movements.

**공기 청정기 (air purifier): PARTIAL / GENUINE for one product**
- 1990s: The Wisconsin Center for Space Automation and Robotics, with NASA Marshall, built a device that uses UV light and titanium dioxide to break down ethylene gas. The aim was to keep plants growing in space from ripening and rotting too fast. It became a commercial air purifier (Airocide). — [NASA Spinoff 2018 cg_2](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/Spinoff2018/cg_2.html), via [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_spin-off_technologies)
- Caution: air purifiers in general existed before. Only this photocatalytic type is NASA-linked. I did not fetch the 2018 page directly.
- Kid story: Plants grown in a spaceship give off a gas that makes fruit spoil fast. Scientists used light to break that gas apart, and now the same idea cleans air on Earth.

**화재 경보기 (Skylab smoke detector): PARTIAL (NASA did NOT invent it)**
- NASA's FAQ answers "No" to "Did NASA invent the smoke detector?" NASA made a smoke detector "with adjustable sensitivity as a part of a Skylab project" (1970s). A Honeywell spinoff detector was sold for some years and is no longer available. — [NASA Spinoff FAQ](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/faq)
- Earlier smoke detectors existed. Walter Jaeger (Swiss) is credited with an early one in the 1930s. — [Inventors Digest via search](https://inventorsdigest.com/?p=15931) (secondary)
- The NASA Administrator ordered communications staff to vet claims like the smoke detector one. — [NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20070019744)
- Recommendation: if the textbook implies NASA invented it, the lesson should say "NASA가 스카이랩에서 감도를 조절할 수 있게 개선했다".

**적외선 체온계 (ear thermometer): GENUINE (JPL astronomy sensor know-how)**
- Diatek Corporation joined JPL's Technology Affiliates Program in the late 1980s. JPL helped adapt infrared sensor technology, used for missions such as the IRAS infrared astronomy satellite, into the Diatek Model 7000 ear thermometer. It was introduced in 1991 and measures in under 2 seconds. — [JPL blog "Slice of History"](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/blog/2013/5/infrared-ear-thermometer); [NASA Spinoff](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9433)
- Kid story: Astronomers measure how hot stars are from the infrared light they give off. The same sensor idea reads the infrared light from your eardrum to tell your temperature in seconds.

**정수기 (water purification): PARTIAL**
- 2000 and 2002: NASA Johnson SBIR contracts with Argonide produced NanoCeram filter media for recycling water on long spaceflights. It removes more than 99.99% of bacteria and viruses, per the company. It was licensed to Ahlstrom in 2006 and is used in bottles and municipal filters. — [NASA Spinoff](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/10583); [Tech Briefs](https://www.techbriefs.com/component/content/article/32681-nasa-enhanced-water-bottles-filter-water-on-the-go)
- The ISS water recovery system recycles sweat, urine and breath moisture into drinking water. Several companies built spinoffs from it. — [NASA Spinoff trend story](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/page/space-age-water-conservation-nasa); [WEF](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2023/09/nasa-solving-water-problems/)
- Caution: water filters in general predate NASA. The "99.99%" figure is a company claim. Avoid quoting the number to students.
- Kid story: On the space station there is no tap, so astronauts must clean and reuse every drop, even sweat. Filters made for that job now clean water on Earth too.

**투명 치아 교정기 (clear ceramic braces): GENUINE (material)**
- Ceradyne and NASA Advanced Ceramics Research developed translucent polycrystalline alumina (TPA). It was originally meant to protect infrared antennae of heat-seeking missile trackers. Unitek (3M Unitek) used it for nearly invisible brackets, on the market about 1987. NASA Spinoff listed it in 1989. — [NASA Spinoff](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9290); [NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20020087639)
- NASA FAQ: yes, transparent ceramic "originally developed by NASA". — [NASA Spinoff FAQ](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/faq)
- Caution: the original purpose was **military missile trackers** with NASA ceramics research, not space exploration. "Invisible aligners" (clear plastic trays) are a different product and not a NASA spinoff. The textbook item is "교정기", so clarify that it means ceramic brackets.

**인공 청각 장치 (cochlear implant): PARTIAL**
- Adam Kissiah, a NASA Kennedy Space Center engineer with hearing loss, worked on a cochlear implant from the mid-1970s using his electronics and sensor know-how. NASA helped him patent it in 1977. He sold the rights to BIOSTIM, Inc., and that product was tested but never reached the market. — [NASA Spinoff 2003 "Hearing Is Believing"](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/10322); [NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20030099659)
- Caution: modern multi-channel cochlear implants were developed independently by other teams. The search snippet did not name them; I recall they include Graeme Clark in Australia and the Hochmairs in Austria, but this is unverified here. — [Wikipedia: Cochlear implant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochlear_implant)
- Recommendation: say "NASA 기술자가 개발에 기여했다", not "NASA가 발명했다".

**카메라 센서 (CMOS image sensor): GENUINE (strongest example)**
- In the early 1990s Eric Fossum's team at JPL invented the CMOS active-pixel sensor to make spacecraft cameras smaller and lower-power, using about 1/100 the power of CCDs. Photobit licensed it in 1995. NASA calls it its most ubiquitous spinoff, in almost every phone camera. — [NASA Spinoff 2017 cg_1](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/Spinoff2017/cg_1.html); [JPL News](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/national-inventors-hall-of-fame-taps-former-jpl-engineer)
- Kid story: Spacecraft cameras must be tiny and use very little electricity. JPL engineers made a "camera on a chip", and now almost every smartphone camera uses the same kind of chip.

**무선 진공청소기 / 무선 공구 (cordless vacuum, Dustbuster): PARTIAL**
- For Apollo, Black & Decker built a battery-powered lunar drill that could reach up to about 3 m (10 ft) deep. It used a computer program to make the motor use very little power. That motor work led to the Dustbuster handheld cordless vacuum in 1979. — [NASA Spinoff](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9520); [Space Foundation](https://www.spacefoundation.org/space_technology_hal/cordless-tools/)
- But NASA's FAQ says cordless power tools are **not** a NASA invention. Black & Decker unveiled them in 1961, before the Apollo work. — [NASA Spinoff FAQ](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/faq)
- Kid story: Astronauts on the Moon had no electric outlet, so they needed a battery drill that saved power. That power-saving motor later went into handheld cordless vacuums.

**무선 헤드폰 (wireless headphones): UNVERIFIED / likely MYTH**
- I found no NASA Spinoff, JPL or NASA history source that credits NASA with wireless headphones or Bluetooth. Searches turned up nothing. Headsets were used in spaceflight, but that is not evidence of origin.

**진공 청소기 (vacuum cleaner in general): MYTH**
- Vacuum cleaners long predate NASA. Only the cordless handheld type has the Apollo drill link above. (No specific source fetched for the pre-NASA history; general knowledge.)

**내진 장치 (seismic dampers, Taylor Devices): GENUINE / PARTIAL**
- From the 1960s and 70s, Taylor Devices worked as a Honeywell subcontractor on a NASA-funded fluidics project. That work led to fluid dampers that controlled Space Shuttle launch-tower swing arms. From the 1990s it sold the same dampers for buildings and bridges in earthquake zones such as San Francisco, Tokyo and Taiwan. By 2015 about 550 structures had them. Seismic dampers were about 60% of company sales. — [NASA Spinoff 2015](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/10832); [NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20150003545); [Space Foundation](https://www.spacefoundation.org/space_technology_hal/seismic-damper-technology/)
- The claim that no building with the dampers was damaged comes from the company and is not independently verified.
- Kid story: When a rocket launches, the tower shakes hard, and arms holding the rocket must swing away smoothly. Oil-filled "shock absorbers" made for that job now absorb earthquake shaking in tall buildings and bridges.

**전자레인지 (microwave oven): MYTH**
- Percy Spencer at Raytheon developed it around 1945 from radar magnetron work. The patent was filed in 1946, and the "Radarange" came out in 1947. That is before NASA (1958). — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/a-brief-history-of-the-microwave-oven)
- No NASA connection found.

**자율 주행 자동차 (autonomous cars): PARTIAL (research collaboration, not origin)**
- 2015: NASA Ames and Nissan started a 5-year partnership. Nissan LEAF test cars used robotics software first written for Ames's K-10 and K-REX planetary rovers. — [NASA image article](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/nissan-test-drives-nasa-space-technology-use-driverless-cars/)
- JPL's GPS-correction software (Real-Time GIPSY) is licensed by John Deere for self-driving farm tractors. — [NASA "Highly Accurate GPS"](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/spacetech/spinoff/Highly_Accurate_GPS_Is_Possible_Thanks_to_NASA); [NASA Spinoff 2017 ee_1](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/Spinoff2017/ee_1.html)
- Caution: self-driving car technology overall (Waymo, Tesla, etc.) was not created by NASA. The Mars rover autonomy story is a good analogy but not an origin.

**GPS: PARTIAL (NASA improved accuracy; DoD built GPS)**
- GPS was started by the U.S. Department of Defense in 1973 (Joint Program Office, Air Force lead). — [Inside GNSS](https://insidegnss.com/40th-anniversary-of-key-dod-decision-on-gps/)
- After GPS became fully operational in 1994, JPL developed GIPSY and Real-Time GIPSY, which correct GPS errors to within inches. It is used for precision farming and for locating 911 callers. — [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/spacetech/spinoff/Highly_Accurate_GPS_Is_Possible_Thanks_to_NASA); [NASA Spinoff](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/node/11028)
- Kid story: GPS satellites were launched by the U.S. military. NASA's JPL then made software that corrects GPS errors, so positions can be accurate to a few centimeters.

### Inferences
- The textbook figure title "일상생활에서 사용하는 우주 탐사 기술" is defensible if read as "space technology used in" rather than "invented by" space exploration.
- Several items came from NASA **aeronautics** (memory foam) or **military-adjacent** research (TPA braces), not from space exploration in the narrow sense.
- For a middle-school page, the three safest "hero" examples are the CMOS camera sensor, the ear thermometer and scratch-resistant lenses. All are clear, NASA-confirmed and close to students' lives.

### Gaps
- Microwave oven and wireless headphones: no NASA source found. They seem to be textbook errors or loose associations, and the user should check which textbook (출판사) lists them and how it phrases them.
- Air purifier (Spinoff 2018) and LASIK (Spinoff 2003) primary pages were cited through Wikipedia, not fetched directly.
- The Space Foundation lists AlterG's Hall of Fame induction as 2026. This looked unusual, but it is consistent with today's date (2026-10).

## Q2. Well-known myths (Velcro, Tang, Teflon, space pen, others)

### Takeaway
NASA's own FAQ says Tang, Velcro and Teflon were invented elsewhere before or apart from NASA. Spaceflight only made them famous. The "million-dollar NASA space pen vs. Russian pencil" story is also a myth: Fisher paid for the pen himself.

### Cited Findings
- **Tang**: General Foods developed it in 1957. John Glenn's 1962 flight made it popular. Not a NASA invention. — [NASA Spinoff FAQ](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/faq)
- **Velcro**: invented in the 1940s by Swiss engineer George de Mestral, inspired by burrs on his dog's fur, and patented in 1955. Apollo use made people link it to NASA. — [NASA Spinoff FAQ](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/faq); [Naked Scientists](https://www.thenakedscientists.com/node/173799)
- **Teflon**: discovered by accident at DuPont in 1938 by Roy Plunkett, who was trying to make a refrigerant. NASA used it in space gear, which raised awareness. — [NASA Spinoff FAQ](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/faq); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna43772079)
- **Space pen**: Paul Fisher developed it with his own money, about $1 million. He offered it to NASA in 1965, and NASA bought about 400 pens in 1967 for $2.95 each. The pen became needed after the Apollo 1 fire because pencil wood and graphite could burn and break off and float. A NASA official said Fisher "has never been under contract to NASA" for pens. — [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/the-write-stuff); [The Space Review](https://thespacereview.com/article/613/1); [Wikipedia: Space Pen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Pen)
- **Other "No" answers on NASA's FAQ**: MRI, cordless power tools (Black & Decker 1961), solar panels (photovoltaic cells from the 19th century), barcodes, quartz clocks (1927), smoke detector (NASA only improved it). — [NASA Spinoff FAQ](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/faq)
- **Microwave oven**: Raytheon, 1945–47, before NASA existed. — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/a-brief-history-of-the-microwave-oven)
- **"Yes" answers on NASA's FAQ**: memory foam, invisible (ceramic) braces, scratch-resistant lenses, emergency "space blankets". — [NASA Spinoff FAQ](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/faq)

### Inferences
- A good classroom activity: "진짜일까, 가짜일까?" cards with Velcro, Tang and Teflon as myths versus the CMOS camera and ear thermometer as genuine. Students learn that "used in space" is not the same as "invented for space".

### Gaps
- Wikipedia says NASA once started its own pen project that was cancelled. This conflicts with the Fisher accounts and is unverified.

## Q3. How many NASA spinoffs exist? Korean examples (KARI)

### Takeaway
NASA says its Spinoff publication has profiled **more than 2,000 spinoffs since 1976**. For Korea, there is no Korean "spinoff" catalog comparable to NASA's. The clearest Korea-specific "everyday" uses are satellite data from 천리안 (weather, fine dust, ocean) and 아리랑 (land mapping, disasters, agriculture), not consumer products.

### Cited Findings
- More than 2,000 spinoffs have been profiled since 1976, when the first full-color Spinoff was published. NASA's Technology Transfer Program dates from 1964. — [spinoff.nasa.gov](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/); [NASA Technology Transfer](https://technology.nasa.gov/); [Spinoff About](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/Spinoff)
- For the 40th anniversary, NASA published a gallery of the 40 spinoffs with the biggest impact. — [NASA Spinoff FAQ](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/faq)
- 천리안 2B호 is described as the world's first satellite to observe air quality from geostationary orbit. It tracks the movement of fine dust and other air pollutants around the Korean Peninsula. — [아이뉴스24 / 항우연 소개 기사](https://www.inews24.com/view/311210) (secondary, Korean news)
- 아리랑 satellite images are used for land development, GIS (maps), and disaster and environment monitoring. 아리랑 3호 has 0.7 m resolution, and 아리랑 5호's radar (SAR) sees through clouds and at night. — [헤럴드경제 2026-06-07](https://www.heraldk.com/article/2026060718080872054) (secondary)
- KARI launched 차세대중형위성 1호 in 2021 to transfer satellite technology to private industry. — [헤럴드경제](https://www.heraldk.com/article/2026060718080872054)
- Korean press overview of "우주기술 스핀오프": [전자신문 2016](https://www.etnews.com/20160708000194) (not fetched in detail)

### Inferences
- For a Korean classroom, "천리안 위성으로 일기 예보·미세먼지 정보를 본다" is a concrete, true everyday link. It is a use of space technology, but it is not a "spinoff product".

### Gaps
- I found no official KARI list of consumer spinoff products. The agriculture use of 아리랑 images (crop monitoring) was not confirmed with a primary KARI or 농촌진흥청 source in this pass.
- The exact current NASA spinoff count beyond "more than 2,000" was not available.
